[[jtag-vs-others]]
=== JTAG versus other interfaces

No single access method is best in every situation. The right choice depends on
design stage, access constraints, speed, and what the target must support.

.Comparison of common device-access methods.
[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Strengths |Limitations |Typical use
|JTAG |Powerful debug, scan access, recovery, board test |More pins, more setup, hardware access required |Bring-up, debug, manufacturing
|DFU |Simple firmware update over USB |Usually limited to supported bootloader paths |End-user or service updates
|UART bootloader |Cheap, easy, widely understood |Slower, often feature-limited |Low-cost update and recovery
|SWD |Fewer pins than JTAG, strong debug support |ARM-focused, not a general JTAG replacement |ARM debug and flashing
|ISP |Simple in-circuit programming for some parts |Highly device-specific |MCU programming in production
|===

==== JTAG versus DFU

Use DFU when the device already provides a USB bootloader and the workflow is
meant to be simple for end users or service personnel.

Use JTAG when you need deeper access, board-level test capability, or a way to
recover a device before the application firmware is functional.

In short: DFU is often about convenience; JTAG is often about control.

==== JTAG versus UART bootloaders

UART bootloaders are common and useful, especially on low-cost systems. They are
often easier to expose in a minimal product, but they typically depend on the
bootloader itself being alive.

JTAG can reach the target even when the bootloader is damaged or absent.

==== JTAG versus SWD

Serial Wire Debug (SWD) is a two-wire debug protocol used mainly with ARM
microcontrollers. It is closely related to JTAG in purpose, but it uses fewer
pins and a simpler physical interface.

When a chip offers both, SWD is often preferred for compact ARM designs. JTAG
remains important when the device family, board test strategy, or tooling
depends on the full JTAG feature set.

==== JTAG versus ISP

ISP usually means in-system programming through a vendor-specific interface or
boot path. It is often simple and efficient for a specific chip family, but it
is rarely as general as JTAG.

JTAG is more standardized across devices, while ISP is often more convenient
inside a single ecosystem.

[[jtag-decision]]
=== How to choose

A practical rule is:

* choose DFU or UART bootloaders for simple update workflows
* choose SWD for compact ARM debug if the device supports it well
* choose JTAG when you need broad access, board test, or robust recovery

JTAG is usually the better choice when the question is "Can I access the chip
at all?" rather than "Can I ship a user-friendly update path?"

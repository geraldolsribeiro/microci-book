= Introduction

=== Why this book exists

This book is for readers who want to build embedded software with a workflow
that remains practical, reproducible, and under their control.

It is also for readers who need to justify that workflow in environments where
internet access is limited, external services are undesirable, or the
engineering process must remain auditable. In those cases, the usual cloud CI
story is often a poor fit. The reasons are explained in more detail in
<<Air-gapped environments>>.

microCI matters when a project must keep working without depending on cloud
services, external accounts, or a strong network connection. That is common in
restricted labs, on isolated networks, and in military or defense settings.

This book is meant to show that embedded development does not have to be a
collection of fragile steps, manual fixes, and unrepeatable builds. A project
can remain disciplined, traceable, and practical even when it must run inside a
controlled environment.

For teams in defense, industrial, or other sensitive contexts, that is more
than convenience. It is a way to reduce operational risk and keep ownership of
the workflow.

=== Why embedded CI is hard

Embedded CI is harder than typical application CI because the build often spans
multiple domains at once: host tools, cross-compilation, board-specific code,
flashing steps, and hardware verification.

Unlike a pure software project, a successful pipeline is not finished when the
binary compiles. It may still need to produce UF2 images, capture serial logs,
load firmware onto a board, or validate behavior with physical hardware.

Those extra steps make the workflow powerful, but they also make it easier to
break, harder to reproduce, and more dependent on the exact environment.

=== Why microCI

microCI is the book's answer to that problem. It keeps the pipeline in plain
text, makes each step visible, and lets the same workflow run in a controlled
way across different machines.

That matters when the environment is isolated, when the toolchain must be
self-contained, or when the team wants every step to remain reviewable in the
repository.

=== Why the Raspberry Pi Pico family

The Pico family is small enough to be approachable and capable enough to show
real embedded concerns. It gives the book a practical platform without turning
the hardware itself into the main subject.

=== Why modern C++

Modern C++ lets the examples stay expressive, safer, and closer to how real
firmware can be organized today. The goal is not language novelty; it is clearer
code that still fits embedded constraints.

=== What the reader will learn

By the end of the book, the reader should be able to reason about firmware
structure, toolchains, peripheral access, timing, testing, and the role of
microCI in a controlled development environment.

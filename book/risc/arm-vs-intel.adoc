=== RISC compared with ARM and Intel

RISC is a design approach, while ARM and Intel are names you are more likely to
see as ecosystems or instruction-set families in real products.

ARM systems are commonly found in embedded devices and mobile hardware.
They are often efficient, well-supported, and practical for firmware where power
use and peripheral integration matter.
RISC-V is newer as a public instruction-set standard and is attractive because
it is open, modular, and easy to study.

Intel x86 systems are usually associated with PCs, servers, and heavier desktop
software.
They are powerful, but they also tend to bring more complexity than most small
embedded projects need.

For embedded readers, the main difference is not just speed.
It is the shape of the ecosystem:

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Family |Typical strengths |Typical fit
|ARM |Mature embedded ecosystem, strong vendor support, many boards |Most embedded and mobile-style devices
|RISC-V |Open standard, modular design, easy to study and extend |Learning, new designs, and open hardware work
|Intel x86 |Broad compatibility, large software ecosystem |PCs, servers, and general-purpose computing
|===

The right choice depends on the project, not the slogan.
